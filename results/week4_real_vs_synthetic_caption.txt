Figure: Geographic location extraction on synthetic claims vs real HumAID crisis posts.

The same spaCy geographic-entity extractor was run over 100 manually written synthetic claims and 100 real, manually labelled HumAID disaster tweets. Real crisis-language performance is substantially lower than synthetic performance across every metric: exact/manual match rate falls from 85% to 52%, extraction rate from 80% to 64%, while the missing-location rate rises from 9% to 29%. Because the extractor is identical, the gap is attributable to the text itself: real posts hide places in hashtags, abbreviations, nicknames, misspellings, and multi-location or consequence-only language. This demonstrates the importance of evaluating geographic extraction on noisy real-world text rather than on controlled synthetic claims, which otherwise overstate how well the pipeline's location step works. Values are read directly from data/week4_real_vs_synthetic.csv; no values are fabricated.
